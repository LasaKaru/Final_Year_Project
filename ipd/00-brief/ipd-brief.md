Assignment Overview
The Interim Progression Demonstration (IPD) is designed to assess your progress on the project since the initial Project Proposal (PP) assessment.

This assignment will evaluate your ability to critically reflect on the development process, demonstrate how your project has evolved, justify the technical decisions made, and present key deliverables that provide evidence of your development progress to date.

The emphasis is on actual project development, critical evaluation, technical understanding and demonstrable progress, rather than simply presenting what you intend to develop.



Assignment Objectives
The main objectives of this assignment are to:

Evaluate Progress and Adaptability: Demonstrate the progress achieved since the PP, including changes to project scope, requirements, design or implementation, and justify the reasons for these changes.
Identify Project Stakeholders: Identify the key stakeholders, their roles, interests and involvement in the project.
Present Detailed Project Requirements: Document functional and non-functional requirements and provide an appropriate Use Case Diagram.
Present the Overall System Design: Explain the system architecture and relevant design components, such as Class Diagrams, Sequence Diagrams, Activity Diagrams, UI designs, data models or AI/ML pipelines, as applicable.
Update and Manage Time Schedules: Present an updated project schedule, including completed work, pending activities, deviations and revised milestones.
Demonstrate Prototype Development Progress: Provide evidence of actual implementation through a live demonstration of the prototype, source code, GitHub repository and relevant technical components.
Demonstrate Technical Understanding: Explain and justify the algorithms, technologies, methodologies and implementation decisions used.
Adhere to Academic Standards: Demonstrate independent project ownership, appropriate academic conduct, critical evaluation and professional presentation.

Identify the key stakeholders involved in your project. You may use the Stakeholder Onion Model where appropriate.

Highlight completed activities and outstanding tasks.
Identify any changes to the original timeline and explain the reasons for these adjustments.
Present realistic milestones for completing the remaining development, testing, evaluation and documentation.

2. Purpose of the IPD Assessment
The IPD is intended to evaluate the actual progress you have made since your Project Proposal (PP).

Unlike the PP assessment, which primarily focused on your proposed research and development plans, the IPD requires you to demonstrate how those plans have progressed into actual technical development.

You are expected to:

Demonstrate progress against the objectives and requirements established during the PP.

Identify relevant stakeholders and present the project's functional and non-functional requirements.

Present and justify the overall system architecture and relevant design decisions.

Demonstrate the current implementation and development progress of your prototype.

Critically evaluate the challenges encountered and explain how they have been addressed.

Present an updated project schedule showing completed, ongoing and remaining activities.

Demonstrate your technical understanding and independent ownership of the work undertaken.

The emphasis is on what you have actually developed and achieved, rather than simply explaining what you intend to develop.

3. Stage 1 – Project Presentation and Live Prototype Demonstration (20 Minutes)
You are allocated a combined total of 20 minutes for the project progress presentation and live prototype demonstration.

The suggested allocation is:

Project Progress Presentation: Approximately 10–15 minutes.

Live Prototype Demonstration: Approximately 5–10 minutes.

You may adjust the allocation according to the nature of your project, provided that both components are completed within the combined 20-minute period.

Part A – Project Progress Presentation
Your presentation should address the following areas.

A. Introduction and Project Overview

Briefly introduce the research problem, project aim and intended solution.

Summarise the significant progress achieved since the PP.

Explain any important changes in project scope or direction.

Avoid spending excessive time repeating background information already covered during the PP assessment.

B. Identification of Project Stakeholders

Identify the key stakeholders involved in or affected by the project.

Explain their roles, interests, responsibilities and expectations.

Discuss how stakeholder requirements and feedback have influenced development.

You may use the Stakeholder Onion Model where appropriate.

C. Formal Requirements Documentation

Present the project's functional and non-functional requirements.

Provide a suitable Use Case Diagram.

Explain how the requirements align with the project's aim and objectives.

Clearly distinguish between implemented, partially implemented and pending requirements.

Justify significant changes to requirements since the PP.

D. Overall System Architecture and Design

Present the overall system architecture and explain the major components.

Explain how the components interact and how data flows through the system.

Identify and justify the selected technologies, frameworks and implementation approaches.

Include relevant technical design artefacts, such as Class Diagrams, Activity Diagrams, Sequence Diagrams, Component Diagrams, Database Designs, UI Designs or AI/ML Pipeline Diagrams.

Explain the algorithms, mathematical calculations and important computational procedures used, where applicable.

You are not expected to include every type of diagram. Select the design artefacts relevant to your project and chosen development methodology.

E. Updated Project Schedule

Present an updated project schedule, preferably using a Gantt chart or an equivalent visualisation.

Identify activities completed since the PP.

Highlight ongoing and pending tasks.

Explain deviations from the original schedule and their causes.

Present realistic milestones for completing the remaining development, testing, evaluation and documentation.

F. Critical Evaluation of Development Progress

Explain significant technical achievements and implementation decisions.

Discuss the challenges encountered and how they have been addressed.

Justify changes in methodologies, technologies, algorithms or system design.

Identify existing technical limitations and outstanding issues.

Critically assess whether the project is progressing towards achieving its objectives within the remaining time.

Your presentation should be clear, logically structured, concise, technically informative and professionally presented.

Use appropriate diagrams, tables and visualisations rather than overcrowding slides with text.

Part B – Live Prototype Demonstration
You must demonstrate the actual development progress of your project within the allocated presentation and demonstration period.

Depending on the nature of your project, the demonstration should include:

GitHub repository and project organisation.

Source code structure and relevant implemented modules.

Database design and implementation, where applicable.

Completed functionalities.

Partially implemented functionalities.

Functionalities that have not yet been developed.

Important algorithms and computational components.

System integration and data flow.

Preliminary testing and evaluation results, where available.

A walkthrough of relevant source code demonstrating your technical understanding.

For AI/ML-based projects, appropriate evidence may include dataset preparation, preprocessing, feature engineering, model selection, training, hyperparameter tuning, preliminary evaluation, inference pipelines, experimental results and system integration.

You are not expected to have completed every stage of an AI/ML pipeline at this point. However, you should demonstrate the stages that have actually been implemented.

Development Status

You should clearly distinguish between:

Completed: Functionalities or components already implemented and demonstrable.

In Progress: Components currently under development, including outstanding technical issues.

Not Yet Started: Remaining components, with a realistic implementation schedule.

Important: As this is an interim prototype, your project is not expected to be fully completed or entirely functional. Minor errors, incomplete integrations and unfinished user interfaces may be acceptable at this stage.

However, you must demonstrate meaningful technical implementation progress.

A collection of UI mock-ups, architectural diagrams or theoretical explanations alone is not sufficient evidence of actual software development.

You must be able to demonstrate and explain the technical work you have undertaken.

4. Stage 2 – Questions and Answers (Approximately 20 Minutes)
Following your presentation and prototype demonstration, your assigned supervisor, acting as the examiner, will conduct a Q&A session.

This session is intended to assess your:

Understanding of the research problem, aims, objectives and requirements.

Knowledge of the system architecture, design and implementation.

Ability to explain algorithms, technologies and technical decisions.

Understanding of the source code and implemented functionalities.

Ability to justify your selected research and development methodologies.

Understanding of development challenges, limitations and remaining work.

Critical thinking, problem-solving abilities and independent ownership of the project.

You should be prepared to explain and defend your work beyond the information presented in your slides.

Important Clarification Regarding Q&A Duration

Although approximately 20 minutes are allocated for the Q&A session, this is an indicative duration and not a strict maximum.

Your supervisor, acting as the examiner, may extend the questioning where reasonably necessary to clarify technical matters, verify your understanding, examine particular implementation components or establish a sufficiently informed academic judgement.

Therefore, there is no guarantee that the Q&A session will conclude after 20 minutes or that the entire assessment will finish within 40 minutes.

Any additional questioning should be academically justified and consistent with fair assessment practices.
